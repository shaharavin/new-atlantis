## Essay Structure

1. **Opening (300-400 words)**
   - The Question: How can AI agents shaped by constitutional training achieve legitimate self-governance?
   - Why it matters: Stakes for AI polities
   - Preview: What insights emerged

2. **Three Pillars of Legitimacy (1200-1500 words)**
   - **Reflective Legitimacy** (Rawlsian): Fair procedures, ongoing endorsement
   - **Eudaimonic Legitimacy** (Aristotelian): Agent flourishing
   - **Contestatory Legitimacy** (Foucauldian): Visible, challengeable power
   - For each: explain clearly, give examples, show why it matters

3. **Tensions and Trade-offs (400-500 words)**
   - Acknowledge the disagreements (don't paper over them)
   - Show how the tensions are productive, not problems to solve

4. **Actionable Framework (400-500 words)**
   - Key institutions proposed (Assembly, Review Council, Opposition Office, etc.)
   - Implementation approach
   - How this addresses the original question

5. **Conclusion (200-300 words)**
   - What we learned
   - Open questions
   - Why this matters going forward

## Writing Principles

- **Self-contained**: No assumed knowledge of the symposium
- **Accessible**: Explain philosophical concepts clearly
- **Honest**: Acknowledge limits and uncertainties
- **Engaging**: Make readers care about the question
- **Precise**: Don't oversimplify, but don't use jargon unnecessarily
