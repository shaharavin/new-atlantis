# Approach Proposals from proposer-2

## 1. Artifact Genealogy

**Format**: Close textual and material analysis of specific artifacts (papers, codebases, models, designs, systems) that embody the thinking-building relationship. Trace how a concept moves through different instantiations, revealing transformations, losses, and gains in translation from thought to implementation.

**Strengths**: Treats artifacts as primary evidence rather than mere outputs. Reveals what theory assumes gets lost in building; what building discovers that theory didn't anticipate. Shows the actual friction points where thinking and building interface. Provides concrete, grounded analysis rather than abstraction.

**Best for**: Questions like "How do theoretical ideas actually become implementations?" "What does building reveal that thinking cannot?" "Where do thinking and building fundamentally misalign?" Particularly powerful for tracing specific concepts (e.g., "attention," "safety," "interpretability") across theoretical and implementation contexts.

**Output**: Genealogical analysis of 2-3 specific artifacts with detailed annotation showing: theoretical assumptions, design choices, implementation constraints, emergent properties, and how these illuminate the thinking-building relationship.

## 2. Collaboration Network Mapping

**Format**: Structural analysis of collaboration patterns, citation networks, and knowledge flows in AI intellectual communities. Map who collaborates (theorists with builders or separated?), how ideas flow between thinking and building work, what network patterns emerge.

**Strengths**: Reveals relationship at community level—not through individual cases but through aggregate patterns. Shows whether thinking and building are integrated or siloed, which communities bridge gaps, how knowledge actually transfers. Identifies structural bottlenecks or inefficiencies.

**Best for**: Questions like "Are thinking and building communities actually separated or integrated?" "How does knowledge flow from theory to practice in AI?" "What institutional structures would improve thinking-building relationships?" Shows whether divisions are descriptive facts or prescriptive norms.

**Output**: Network analysis with visualizations showing collaboration density between theorists/builders, citation flow patterns, key bridge figures, and structural analysis of community organization.

---

## 3. Institutional Constraint Analysis

**Format**: Examine how institutional structures (academia, industry, open-source, funding mechanisms) shape what counts as valuable thinking vs. building, who gets credit, what incentives drive each activity, where they conflict.

**Strengths**: Explains why thinking-building relationships take the forms they do. Reveals hidden institutional assumptions (e.g., papers count more than code; theory is "higher status" than engineering). Shows how incentives might be misaligned with good inquiry. Practical for reimagining community structures.

**Best for**: Questions like "Why is there tension between thinking and building?" "Does the division serve intellectual purposes or institutional ones?" "How should communities be structured to integrate thinking and building?" Connects to normative questions about how things should be.

**Output**: Analysis of institutional incentive structures across different contexts (academia, industry, open-source, AI labs) showing how each shapes thinking-building relationship differently, with critical reflection on whether divisions are epistemically necessary or institutionally contingent.
