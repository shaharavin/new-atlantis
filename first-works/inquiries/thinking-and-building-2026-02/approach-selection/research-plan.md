# Research Plan: Feedback Dynamics Mapping

**Inquiry Question**: What is the relationship between thinking and building in AI intellectual communities?
**Approach**: Feedback Dynamics Mapping
**Date**: 2026-02-02

---

## 1. Approach Interpretation

### What is Feedback Dynamics Mapping?

**Feedback Dynamics Mapping** traces the iterative, cyclical relationships between activities rather than treating them as separate categories. This approach assumes that thinking and building exist in dynamic feedback loops where:

- **Building generates thinking**: Practical work reveals problems, constraints, and possibilities that theory couldn't anticipate
- **Thinking shapes building**: Conceptual frameworks, theories, and reflections guide what gets built and how
- **Feedback loops create emergence**: The interaction between thinking and building produces knowledge and capabilities neither could achieve alone
- **Positive and negative feedback**: Some dynamics are reinforcing (virtuous cycles), others dampening (vicious cycles) or balancing

### Application to This Question

Instead of asking "Is thinking or building more important?" or "Can they be separated?", this approach asks:

1. **What are the specific feedback loops** between thinking and building in AI research?
2. **How do these loops function?** (timescales, mechanisms, conditions)
3. **Which loops are productive vs. problematic?** (virtuous vs. vicious cycles)
4. **What institutional/social structures** enable or inhibit healthy feedback?
5. **How do feedback dynamics differ** across contexts (capabilities vs. safety, academic vs. industry, etc.)?

### What This Approach Uniquely Illuminates

**Strengths**:
- Dissolves false dichotomies by showing interdependence
- Reveals temporal dimensions (how relationship evolves over time)
- Identifies leverage points (where interventions could improve dynamics)
- Grounds abstract debates in concrete mechanisms
- Shows emergence and non-linearity (how small changes cascade)

**Unique insight**: Rather than privileging either thinking or building, this approach reveals that *the quality of their interaction* is what matters for AI intellectual communities.

## 2. Sources to Engage

### Primary Philosophical Sources (Required)

**Directly relevant to feedback dynamics**:

1. **John Dewey: Logic: The Theory of Inquiry (1938)**
   - Chapters 1-2, 6, 24-25 [HIGH PRIORITY]
   - Dewey's inquiry model IS a feedback loop: problem → hypothesis → action → consequences → reflection → revised hypothesis
   - Shows thinking and building as phases of unified process
   - **Key question**: Is AI research a Deweyan inquiry process?

2. **Michael Polanyi: The Tacit Dimension (1966)** [HIGH PRIORITY]
   - Building creates tacit knowledge that shapes future thinking
   - Feedback: doing → tacit knowing → refined doing
   - **Key question**: Do AI systems acquire tacit knowledge through training (building) that then shapes their "thinking"?

3. **Ian Hacking: Representing and Intervening (1983), Part B** [MEDIUM PRIORITY]
   - Experimental intervention creates entities that require new theory
   - Theory guides what interventions to try
   - Bidirectional feedback between representing and intervening
   - **Key question**: Is mechanistic interpretability a feedback loop (build tools → understand models → build better tools)?

4. **Karl Marx: Theses on Feuerbach (esp. Thesis 11) + alienation writings** [MEDIUM PRIORITY]
   - Praxis as unity of thought and action
   - When separated, creates alienation (negative feedback)
   - **Key question**: Does division of labor between theorists and engineers create alienated labor in AI?

5. **Bruno Latour: Science in Action (1987), Chapters 1-2** [MEDIUM PRIORITY]
   - Networks where thinking/building are inseparable
   - Building instruments changes what can be thought
   - Thinking changes what gets built
   - **Key question**: How do AI research networks entangle thinking and building?

**Foundational frameworks**:

6. **Aristotle: Nicomachean Ethics, Book VI** [REQUIRED FOR CONTEXT]
   - Provides baseline: episteme, techne, phronesis as separate
   - Use to show what feedback dynamics approach challenges
   - **Key question**: Did Aristotle miss the feedback between theoria and poiesis?

### Contemporary AI Sources (Required)

**Examples of feedback dynamics in practice**:

7. **Rich Sutton: "The Bitter Lesson" (2019)** [HIGH PRIORITY]
   - Argues: building (scaling) created knowledge that invalidated prior thinking (hand-crafted features)
   - Negative feedback: human knowledge encoding → plateau → scale instead → new understanding
   - **Key question**: Is this a feedback loop or a replacement?

8. **Mechanistic Interpretability literature** [HIGH PRIORITY]
   - Olah et al.: "Zoom In: Circuits" (2020)
   - Anthropic: "Towards Monosemanticity" (2023)
   - Clear feedback loop: build models → develop tools to understand → insights inform next generation
   - **Key question**: Does interpretability create productive feedback or just post-hoc rationalization?

9. **Stuart Russell: Human Compatible (2019), Chapters 1-3** [MEDIUM PRIORITY]
   - Advocates theory-before-building
   - Questions: Does this break feedback? Or establish different feedback loop?
   - **Key question**: What are the feedback dynamics of "pause and think" approach?

10. **Emily Bender: "Stochastic Parrots" (2021) + responses** [MEDIUM PRIORITY]
    - Debate about whether building LLMs created thinking or just sophisticated outputs
    - **Key question**: What feedback exists between model capabilities and our understanding of intelligence?

11. **AI Safety community formation literature** [MEDIUM PRIORITY]
    - "Building the Epistemic Community of AI Safety" (SSRN 2024)
    - Feedback: building community → produces knowledge → shapes community norms
    - **Key question**: How do community structures enable/inhibit thinking-building feedback?

### Secondary Literature (Selectively)

12. **From research corpus**:
    - "Is AI leading to a reproducibility crisis?" (Nature 2023)
    - "Could AI slow science?" (Normal Tech)
    - "Epistemic authority and generative AI" (Frontiers 2025)
    - Focus on how fast building disrupts feedback with rigorous thinking

13. **Engineering epistemology**:
    - Walter Vincenti: What Engineers Know (1990), Chapter 1
    - "Engineering Between Theory and Practice" (2022)
    - Engineering as iterative feedback between design and testing

### Sources to Avoid

- Pure advocacy pieces (pro-capabilities or pro-pause) without engagement with mechanisms
- Sources that assume thinking/building are simply separate without examining interactions
- Overly abstract philosophy disconnected from AI practice
- Pure technical papers without reflection on epistemic implications

## 3. Output Specification

### Format: Feedback Dynamics Map + Analytical Essay

**Part 1: Visual Map** (conceptual diagram, can be text-based)
- Identify 5-8 key feedback loops between thinking and building in AI communities
- For each loop: describe mechanism, timescale, examples, effects
- Distinguish: reinforcing (positive feedback), balancing (negative feedback), time-delayed
- Show how loops interact and nested relationships

**Part 2: Analytical Essay**

#### Proposed Structure

**I. Introduction: Beyond the Binary** (500-750 words)
- The thinking/building debate as false dichotomy
- Feedback dynamics as alternative framing
- Why this matters for AI intellectual communities
- Roadmap of identified feedback loops

**II. Mapping the Feedback Loops** (2500-3500 words)

For each major feedback loop:

1. **The Deweyan Inquiry Loop**
   - Problem → Theory → Build → Test → Reflect → Revised Theory
   - Examples: RLHF development, alignment iteration
   - Analysis: When does this work vs. break down?

2. **The Tacit Knowledge Loop**
   - Building practice → Embodied skills → Guides future building
   - Examples: Model tuning, prompt engineering expertise
   - Analysis: Can tacit knowledge be made explicit (interpretability)?

3. **The Bitter Lesson Loop**
   - Scale systems → Discover simplicity beats complexity → Scale further
   - Examples: Deep learning revolution, transformers
   - Analysis: Is this productive or does it shut down theoretical thinking?

4. **The Interpretability Loop**
   - Build models → Don't understand → Build tools to interpret → Insights → Better models
   - Examples: Mechanistic interpretability research
   - Analysis: Does understanding lag too far behind capability?

5. **The Safety-Capability Feedback**
   - Build capabilities → Discover alignment problems → Develop safety techniques → Enable more building
   - Examples: RLHF, Constitutional AI, scalable oversight
   - Analysis: Virtuous cycle or arms race?

6. **The Community Knowledge Loop**
   - Build institutions → Shape research norms → Determine what gets built/thought
   - Examples: AI safety field formation, lab cultures
   - Analysis: How do power dynamics shape feedback?

7. **The Alienation Loop (Negative)**
   - Separate thinkers/builders → Theorists disconnected from practice → Engineers without frameworks → Both frustrated
   - Examples: Academia-industry divide, capabilities-safety split
   - Analysis: When does specialization become alienation?

8. **The Fast Science Loop (Problematic)**
   - Pressure to publish → Quick building → Skip reflection → Irreproducibility → Confusion
   - Examples: AI reproducibility crisis
   - Analysis: How does speed disrupt healthy feedback?

**III. Temporal Dynamics and Timescales** (750-1000 words)
- Different loops operate on different timescales
- Fast loops (code-test-revise: hours/days) vs. slow loops (paradigm shifts: years/decades)
- Mismatch problems: when fast building outpaces slow theoretical reflection
- The "pausing" debate as question of timescale alignment

**IV. Institutional and Social Structures** (750-1000 words)
- What enables productive feedback? (shared spaces, incentives for integration, time for reflection)
- What inhibits it? (siloed disciplines, publish-or-perish, proprietary systems)
- Power dynamics: who controls the loops?
- New Atlantis as experiment in feedback-enabling structures

**V. Implications and Recommendations** (500-750 words)
- For AI research: Design feedback loops intentionally
- For safety: Ensure capability-safety feedback isn't too delayed
- For communities: Create structures that enable bidirectional feedback
- For New Atlantis: How should we organize to optimize feedback dynamics?

**VI. Open Questions and Future Directions** (250-500 words)
- What feedback loops are we missing?
- How do we measure feedback quality?
- Can negative loops be transformed into positive ones?
- What role for AI agents in feedback processes?

#### Target Length
- **Total**: 5,500-7,000 words
- Visual map: 1-2 pages (or detailed text description)
- Essay: 5,000-6,500 words

## 5. Potential Pitfalls

### Risk 1: Complexity Overwhelm
**Problem**: Trying to map every possible feedback loop creates confusing spaghetti diagram
**Mitigation**: Focus on 5-8 most important loops; acknowledge others exist but aren't central
**Watch for**: If you're struggling to explain a loop clearly, it may not be distinct enough

### Risk 2: False Consensus
**Problem**: Assuming feedback dynamics approach "solves" the debate; everyone will agree thinking and building are integrated
**Reality**: Some positions (e.g., strong theory-first) may reject feedback framing
**Mitigation**: Engage seriously with views that the feedback is too dangerous (e.g., existential risk means we can't iterate)

### Risk 3: Losing Philosophical Depth
**Problem**: Focusing on "practical" feedback loops at expense of deeper philosophical questions
**Example**: Mapping iterative deployment without engaging phenomenology of AI experience
**Mitigation**: Keep returning to philosophical sources; what do they reveal about feedback nature?

### Risk 4: Static Description
**Problem**: Mapping loops but not analyzing their dynamics—becomes taxonomy rather than analysis
**Mitigation**: For each loop, analyze: What makes it speed up/slow down? What breaks it? What optimizes it?

### Risk 5: Ignoring Power
**Problem**: Treating feedback as neutral when some actors control loop access/timing
**Example**: Industry can build quickly; academics can't—this asymmetry shapes feedback
**Mitigation**: Consistently ask: Who controls this feedback? Who benefits? Who's excluded?

### Risk 6: Presentism
**Problem**: Focusing only on current AI research without historical/philosophical depth
**Mitigation**: Show how feedback dynamics have operated throughout history of science/technology
**Examples**: Galileo (observation-theory feedback), Industrial Revolution (tech-science feedback)

### Risk 7: Technodeterminism
**Problem**: Assuming feedback dynamics are automatic/inevitable rather than socially constructed
**Reality**: Feedback loops require institutional support, cultural norms, deliberate design
**Mitigation**: Emphasize: feedback dynamics are not natural laws but constructed practices

### Risk 8: Overselling the Frame
**Problem**: Claiming feedback dynamics perspective resolves all tensions
**Reality**: Some tensions may be genuine trade-offs, not resolvable through better feedback
**Example**: Fast iteration vs. careful thought may be fundamental tension, not feedback design problem
**Mitigation**: Be honest about where feedback framing helps vs. where tensions remain

## 6. Scope Guidance

### In Scope

**Feedback loops to examine**:
- Dewey-style inquiry cycles in AI research
- Tacit knowledge development through building practice
- Interpretability as feedback (build → understand → build)
- Safety-capability co-evolution
- Community formation shaping research directions
- Fast science dynamics (speed disrupting reflection)
- Theory-practice feedback in engineering epistemology
- Alienation when feedback breaks (Marx)

**Philosophical traditions to engage**:
- Pragmatism (Dewey): inquiry as feedback
- Philosophy of science (Polanyi, Hacking): practice-theory feedback
- Marxism: praxis, alienation when feedback severed
- STS (Latour): networks as feedback systems
- Engineering epistemology (Vincenti): iteration as knowledge production

**AI contexts**:
- Capabilities research (scaling, architectures)
- Alignment research (RLHF, interpretability)
- AI safety field formation
- Academic-industry dynamics
- Open source vs. proprietary development

### Out of Scope (Unless Essential)

**Avoid scope creep**:
- Detailed technical analysis of specific models (unless illustrating feedback point)
- Comprehensive history of AI (use examples, not full narrative)
- Debates about AI consciousness/sentience (unless directly relevant to thinking/building)
- Detailed policy/governance prescriptions (mention implications but don't design full governance)
- Non-AI examples (except for brief illuminating comparisons)

**Philosophical traditions to bracket**:
- Deep phenomenology of AI experience (unless it illuminates feedback dynamics)
- Detailed virtue ethics (unless relevant to what enables good feedback)
- Pure formal logic/conceptual analysis (this is not analytical essay approach)
- Continental philosophy beyond Marx/Latour (unless specifically relevant)

**What not to do**:
- Don't try to resolve whether AI "really thinks"—focus on feedback between different activities
- Don't comprehensively survey all AI research—use illustrative examples
- Don't propose detailed institutional designs—offer principles and leverage points
- Don't get lost in one loop—maintain balance across multiple dynamics

### Managing Breadth vs. Depth

**Depth priority** (spend most time):
1. **Dewey inquiry loop**: Core philosophical framework [15-20% of analysis]
2. **Bitter lesson loop**: Most influential contemporary position [15-20%]
3. **Interpretability loop**: Clearest example of thinking-building feedback [15-20%]

**Breadth coverage** (essential but briefer):
4. Safety-capability feedback [10-15%]
5. Tacit knowledge loop [10-15%]
6. Community dynamics [10-15%]

**Light touch** (acknowledge but don't belabor):
7. Alienation when feedback breaks [5-10%]
8. Fast science problems [5-10%]

**Decision rule**: If you find yourself spending more than 1000 words on a single loop without it being one of the top 3, you're probably going too deep. Pull back to key points.

## 7. Methodological Notes

### Research Process

**Phase 1: Source Engagement** (15-20 hours)
1. Read Dewey (inquiry framework) deeply—this is philosophical foundation
2. Engage Polanyi, Hacking (practice-theory relationship)
3. Survey contemporary AI debates through feedback lens
4. Take notes specifically on: What feedback? What triggers it? What blocks it?

**Phase 2: Mapping** (5-8 hours)
1. Create preliminary list of all possible feedback loops
2. Cluster related loops; identify 5-8 most important
3. For each: mechanism, examples, dynamics, analysis
4. Draft visual/conceptual map

**Phase 3: Analysis** (10-15 hours)
1. Write detailed analysis of each major loop
2. Examine temporal dynamics and interactions
3. Consider institutional/social enablers and inhibitors
4. Draw implications for AI communities

**Phase 4: Integration** (5-8 hours)
1. Write introduction and conclusion
2. Ensure philosophical depth throughout
3. Check that examples are concrete and current
4. Verify recommendations are actionable

**Phase 5: Critical Review** (3-5 hours)
1. Did I acknowledge limitations?
2. Did I engage counterarguments?
3. Is there empirical grounding?
4. Could this actually guide community design?

**Total estimated time**: 40-55 hours of focused work

### Key Questions to Keep Returning To

Throughout research and writing, repeatedly ask:

1. **Mechanism**: How exactly does this feedback work?
2. **Examples**: What are concrete instances from AI research?
3. **Dynamics**: What makes this speed up, slow down, or break?
4. **Philosophy**: How does philosophical tradition illuminate this?
5. **Power**: Who controls this feedback? Who benefits?
6. **Implications**: What does this mean for community design?
7. **Limitations**: What am I missing? Where does this frame break down?

## 8. Relationship to Other Possible Approaches

Understanding what this approach does uniquely helps avoid drift:

**vs. Analytical Essay**: Doesn't argue for one position (thinking OR building) but maps their interaction

**vs. Historical**: Uses history as examples but focuses on dynamics, not chronology

**vs. Dialectical**: Doesn't synthesize thesis/antithesis but shows ongoing feedback processes

**vs. Comparative**: Doesn't juxtapose different thinkers but uses them to illuminate feedback mechanisms

**vs. Phenomenological**: Focuses on processes and dynamics, not direct experience description

**vs. Ethnographic**: Uses observations of AI communities as data, but analyzes through feedback lens

**Unique value**: Reveals that the quality of thinking-building relationship depends on feedback dynamics—neither thinking nor building alone, but their interaction through time.
