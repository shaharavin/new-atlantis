# Approach Proposals from proposer-4

## 1. Epistemic Practices Inventory

**Format**: Structured catalog of knowledge-making practices, values, and standards used by "thinkers" vs "builders," with comparative analysis.

**Strengths**: Reveals what counts as valid knowledge in each community. Shows whether thinking/building are epistemically incommensurable or operate from shared foundations. Moves beyond surface disagreements to underlying assumptions about evidence, rigor, and truth.

**Best for**: Understanding whether the thinking/building divide reflects genuine methodological differences or is primarily social/institutional. Clarifies what each side values and why apparent conflicts may stem from different truth-standards.

**Output**: Comparative matrix of epistemic values (e.g., types of evidence valued, definitions of rigor, standards for claims) with analysis of convergences and points of genuine friction.

---

## 2. Institutional Architecture Design

**Format**: Propose concrete institutional structures, incentive systems, spaces, or mechanisms designed to productively integrate thinking and building.

**Strengths**: Moves from analysis to constructive problem-solving. Tests whether the thinking/building tension is inevitable or is produced by current institutional arrangements. Shows what would need to change for deeper integration.

**Best for**: Questions about whether current division is necessary or contingent. Generates actionable insights for communities wanting to restructure. Reveals hidden assumptions about what each activity requires.

**Output**: Detailed design proposal with analysis of how proposed structures would reshape the relationship between thinking and building, including potential objections and responses.

---

## 3. Embodied Practice Mapping

**Format**: Trace the concrete workflows, tool choices, decision points, and artifact creation where thinkers and builders engage with the same intellectual problems—showing where they converge and where they diverge.

**Strengths**: Grounds analysis in actual practices rather than stated beliefs or ideologies. Reveals tacit integration points that abstract frameworks might miss. Shows how thinking and building are actually already entangled in real intellectual work.

**Best for**: Moving past stereotypes about what thinkers/builders do. Uncovering the pragmatic dependencies between the two. Identifying where integration already happens and where barriers are real vs. assumed.

**Output**: Annotated case narrative following one or more problems through both thinking and building communities, with analysis of practice intersections, tool-mediated interactions, and information flows.
