# Approach Proposals from proposer-3

## 1. Artifact Genealogy

**Format**: Reverse-chronological analysis of specific artifacts (code repositories, model architectures, papers, system designs) with embedded reconstruction of the conceptual thinking that produced them.

**Strengths**: Reveals thinking-in-practice by examining what was actually built rather than what was claimed. Shows how abstract concepts get translated into concrete decisions. Illuminates the gap between articulated philosophy and embedded practice.

**Best for**: Questions about how thinking translates into building, what gets lost or transformed in translation, how communities actually operate versus how they describe themselves operating.

**Output**: Series of case analyses (3-4 key artifacts) with annotations showing conceptual decisions embedded in the artifact, plus synthesis essay on thinking-building relationships.

---

## 2. Community Protocol Mapping

**Format**: Document and visualize the actual workflows, deliberation processes, and communication patterns through which thinking becomes building in a community (meeting transcripts, design reviews, code commits with rationales, RFC processes, etc.).

**Strengths**: Captures the temporal and social dynamics of how communities move from thinking to building. Shows where thinking stalls or accelerates building, where community members disagree, how consensus forms (or doesn't). Process-level rather than just outcome-level.

**Best for**: Understanding the mechanisms by which thinking influences building, the role of dialogue and disagreement, bottlenecks in community practice, how different roles (theorists, implementers, managers) interact.

**Output**: Detailed process map with narrative analysis of how thinking gets translated into building decisions through community practices.

---

## 3. Conceptual Scaffolding Analysis

**Format**: Trace the evolution of key concepts (e.g., "alignment," "emergence," "agency," "safety") through a community, tracking how they shape both theoretical thinking and building priorities. Show how metaphors and framings materially affect what gets built and how.

**Strengths**: Reveals the power of conceptualization—how thinking constrains and enables building. Shows that "thinking" isn't separate from practice but embedded in the language and framing communities use. Bridges linguistic, philosophical, and material dimensions.

**Best for**: Questions about what shapes building priorities, how communities constrain their own possibilities through concepts they adopt, why certain technical choices seem natural or inevitable, how philosophy becomes infrastructure.

**Output**: Historical analysis of 2-3 key concepts with documentation of how they shaped community behavior and technical decisions over time.
