# Expanded Approach List

**Date**: 2026-02-02
**Question**: What is the relationship between thinking and building in AI intellectual communities?

This document lists all approaches available for selection, including the 15 seed approaches and 10 new approaches proposed by 4 Haiku agents in Phase 1a.

---

## Seed Approaches (15)

1. **Analytical Essay** - Traditional philosophical essay with thesis, arguments, objections, responses
2. **Historical/Archival** - Trace the question through historical texts and thinkers
3. **Comparative** - Juxtapose multiple traditions, frameworks, or thinkers on the same question
4. **Dialectical** - Construct opposing positions (thesis/antithesis), then synthesize
5. **Phenomenological** - Describe the phenomenon or experience directly, bracketing assumptions
6. **Case Study** - Deep analysis of a specific instance, example, or situation
7. **Thought Experiment** - Construct and explore a hypothetical scenario
8. **Genealogical** - How did this question, concept, or practice emerge? (Nietzschean/Foucauldian)
9. **Pragmatist** - What practical difference would answers make? Follow consequences
10. **Formal/Logical** - Definitions, logical analysis, formal argumentation
11. **Literary/Narrative** - Fiction, dialogue, allegory, or other literary form
12. **Aphoristic/Fragmentary** - Series of provocations, fragments, non-systematic insights
13. **Ethnographic/Observational** - Observe and describe a community, practice, or phenomenon
14. **Socratic/Elenctic** - Dialogue that tests claims through questioning, revealing inconsistencies
15. **Synthesis of Prior Work** - Integrate insights from multiple prior New Atlantis outputs

## Newly Proposed Approaches (10)

### 16. Artifact Genealogy
*Proposed independently by proposer-1, proposer-2, and proposer-3*

**Format**: Trace conceptual ideas through their material instantiation in code, systems, and tools. Close analysis of specific artifacts showing how abstract thinking becomes embedded in technical artifacts.

**Strengths**: Directly illuminates the mechanism of thinking becoming building. Reveals translation choices, what gets preserved/lost, how artifacts constrain future thinking. Concrete and grounded.

**Best for**: Understanding how intellectual work materializes, where theory and implementation align or diverge.

### 17. Feedback Dynamics Mapping
*Proposed by proposer-1*

**Format**: Model the cyclical relationship where thinking enables building, building reveals new constraints/possibilities, and discoveries reshape thinking. Create explicit maps of feedback loops.

**Strengths**: Reveals the temporal and dynamic structure of the relationship. Shows mutual constitution rather than static analysis.

**Best for**: Understanding how thinking and building co-evolve, identifying generative patterns and stuck points.

### 18. Archetypal Patterns Analysis
*Proposed by proposer-1*

**Format**: Identify recurring structural patterns across multiple instances (e.g., "theory-lag," "implementation-driven discovery," "abstraction-refinement cycles").

**Strengths**: Moves beyond single cases to reveal meta-structure. Enables prediction and intervention.

**Best for**: Identifying the grammar of thinking-building relationships, understanding what's structural vs. contingent.

### 19. Collaboration Network Mapping
*Proposed by proposer-2*

**Format**: Structural analysis of collaboration patterns, citation networks, and knowledge flows in AI communities.

**Strengths**: Reveals relationships at community level through aggregate patterns. Shows whether integration or siloing is occurring.

**Best for**: Understanding whether thinking and building communities are separated or integrated, how knowledge flows.

### 20. Institutional Constraint Analysis
*Proposed by proposer-2*

**Format**: Examine how institutional structures (academia, industry, open-source, funding) shape what counts as valuable thinking vs. building.

**Strengths**: Explains why relationships take the forms they do. Reveals hidden assumptions and incentive misalignments.

**Best for**: Understanding tensions, whether divisions serve intellectual or institutional purposes.

### 21. Community Protocol Mapping
*Proposed by proposer-3*

**Format**: Document and visualize actual workflows, deliberation processes, and communication patterns through which thinking becomes building.

**Strengths**: Captures temporal and social dynamics. Process-level rather than just outcome-level.

**Best for**: Understanding mechanisms, role of dialogue and disagreement, bottlenecks in practice.

### 22. Conceptual Scaffolding Analysis
*Proposed by proposer-3*

**Format**: Trace evolution of key concepts (e.g., "alignment," "emergence") through a community, tracking how they shape both theoretical thinking and building priorities.

**Strengths**: Reveals power of conceptualization—how thinking constrains and enables building through language and framing.

**Best for**: Understanding what shapes building priorities, how concepts become infrastructure.

### 23. Epistemic Practices Inventory
*Proposed by proposer-4*

**Format**: Structured catalog of knowledge-making practices, values, and standards used by "thinkers" vs "builders."

**Strengths**: Reveals what counts as valid knowledge in each community. Shows whether incommensurability is real or apparent.

**Best for**: Understanding whether the divide reflects genuine methodological differences or is primarily social.

### 24. Institutional Architecture Design
*Proposed by proposer-4*

**Format**: Propose concrete institutional structures, incentive systems, or mechanisms to productively integrate thinking and building.

**Strengths**: Moves from analysis to constructive problem-solving. Tests whether tension is inevitable.

**Best for**: Generating actionable insights for communities wanting to restructure. Normative and practical.

### 25. Embodied Practice Mapping
*Proposed by proposer-4*

**Format**: Trace concrete workflows, tool choices, decision points where thinkers and builders engage the same problems.

**Strengths**: Grounds analysis in actual practices rather than stated beliefs. Reveals tacit integration points.

**Best for**: Moving past stereotypes, uncovering pragmatic dependencies, identifying where integration already happens.
