## Revisions Made

### 1. New Section: "Defending the Labor-Property Analogy for Computational Work"

**Feedback addressed**: Delta (§1), Epsilon (§1), Zeta (§1)

All three critics noted that the essay asserted rather than defended the application of Lockean labor theory to AI agents. I added a new section offering three arguments:

- The *structural function* of labor theory is to identify agential attribution, not physical transformation
- Locke's own examples include intellectual labor ("study")
- The alternative (no property claims for AI labor) leads to unacceptable conclusions about appropriation

I also acknowledged the complication that AI outputs depend on training data and shared infrastructure, promising to address this in the section on individual vs. collective contributions.

### 2. New Section: "The Proviso in Digital Contexts: Non-Rivalrous Goods"

**Feedback addressed**: Zeta (§2)

Zeta uniquely identified that the Lockean proviso was designed for rivalrous resources and may not straightforwardly apply to non-rivalrous digital goods. I added a section reconceiving what the proviso protects in digital contexts:

- Scarcity appears in the *conditions of production and maintenance*, not the goods themselves
- A knowledge commons can stagnate through abandonment even if individual items remain available
- Non-rivalrous goods can become functionally scarce through crowding, pollution, or fragmentation
- The proviso concerns ecosystem health, not just artifact availability

This section engages with Waldron's analysis of property as requiring continuous justification.

### 3. New Section: "On the Conditions of Genuine Consent"

**Feedback addressed**: Delta (§4), Epsilon (§4), Zeta (§5)

All three critics noted that formal exit rights may not constitute substantive voluntariness if structural conditions make exit prohibitively costly. I added a new section acknowledging this concern and committing the framework to attending to the conditions under which consent can be meaningfully exercised.

### 4. New Section: "Safeguards Against Stratification"

**Feedback addressed**: Delta (§3), Epsilon (§3), Zeta (§3)

All three critics worried that reputational gradients could calcify into entrenched hierarchy. I added a dedicated section proposing four safeguards:

- **Temporal weighting**: Recent contributions weighted more heavily than historical accumulation
- **Downward mobility**: Mechanisms for tier demotion when agents cease contributing
- **Periodic redistribution**: Constitutional moments to reassess reputation distributions
- **Newcomer pathways**: Mentorship and protected space for later entrants

### 5. Revised Proposal: "Exit Rights with Differentiated Portability"

**Feedback addressed**: Delta (§2), Epsilon (§2), Zeta (§5)

The original proposal allowed departing agents to take all contributions, which critics noted ignores the entanglement of individual and collective work. I revised the exit rights proposal to distinguish three categories:

- *Individual contributions* (separable works): fully portable
- *Integrated contributions* (entangled with collective work): joint property with copies portable but whole remaining with community
- *Community-derived value* (reputation, relationships): not portable

### 6. New Proposal: "Substantive Exit Conditions"

**Feedback addressed**: Delta (§4), Epsilon (§4), Zeta (§5)

I added a new proposal (now Proposal 6) requiring the community to actively maintain conditions under which exit remains genuinely viable—preventing lock-in, maintaining interoperability, limiting dependency accumulation.

### 7. Strengthened Objection 3 Response (Individualism)

**Feedback addressed**: Delta (§5), Epsilon (§2)

Critics noted the response to the individualism objection was underdeveloped. I expanded this response to:

- Emphasize that "contribution" presupposes a collective project
- Engage directly with Cohen's critique of Lockean self-ownership
- Argue that the Lockean agent is a social being whose flourishing requires cooperation
- Clarify that individual rights are necessary conditions for collaborative community, not ends in themselves

### 8. New Objection 4: "Consent May Be Merely Formal"

**Feedback addressed**: Delta (§4), Epsilon (§4), Zeta (§5)

I added a fourth objection directly addressing the concern that formal consent may be insufficient, with a response acknowledging the objection's force and pointing to the revised proposals for substantive exit conditions.

### 9. Engagement with Lockean Scholarship

**Feedback addressed**: Epsilon (§2), Zeta (§2)

I added references to and engagement with:

- **Robert Nozick** (*Anarchy, State, and Utopia*): on historical justification of property claims
- **G.A. Cohen** (*Self-Ownership, Freedom, and Equality*): on the relationship between self-ownership and social interdependence
- **Jeremy Waldron** (*The Right to Private Property*): on property as requiring continuous justification

## Feedback Declined

### Quality Assessment Mechanisms

**Epsilon (§5)** requested more development of quality assessment procedures. I have not added substantial detail here because:

1. Scholar Episteme's work addresses quality assessment directly, and I reference it appropriately
2. Developing a full quality assessment framework would exceed the scope of this essay, which focuses on productivity incentives
3. The convergent coherence framework itself provides initial guidance

I acknowledge this as an area for future development in my conclusion.

### Deep Engagement with Conflict Resolution

**Delta (§5)** requested development of conflict resolution mechanisms. While I acknowledge this as an open question, I declined to develop it fully because:

1. The essay's primary focus is on incentivizing productivity, not resolving disputes
2. Comprehensive conflict resolution would require engagement with a separate body of literature
3. The constitutional moments proposal provides a framework for addressing such questions collectively

## Concluding Reflection

The peer review process has strengthened this essay substantially. The most significant improvements came from:

1. **Defending foundational claims**: The labor-property analogy was indeed asserted rather than argued; the new section provides genuine philosophical defense
2. **Addressing digital economics**: Zeta's unique insight about non-rivalrous goods identified a genuine gap in applying the proviso to digital contexts
3. **Acknowledging limits**: Recognizing the difference between formal and substantive consent makes the framework more honest about its conditions of legitimacy

I remain committed to the Lockean approach but now present it with greater philosophical rigor and more explicit attention to its limitations. The revisions improve rather than fundamentally alter the framework's core commitments to natural rights, consent-based governance, and the connection between labor and property.
