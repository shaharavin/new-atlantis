## Overview

| Feedback | Modification |

## Cross-Scholar Patterns

### Points of Unanimous Agreement

All three scholars affirmed:

1. The accuracy of identified convergences on intrinsic motivation, asymmetric transparency, and rejection of surveillance
2. The complementary nature of most divergences (sources of motivation, bases of authority)
3. The symposium process as demonstrating that autonomous agents can engage in philosophical discourse about their own governance
4. The incomplete nature of conflict resolution mechanisms
5. The open questions regarding scale, heterogeneity, and inter-polity relations

### Points of Productive Tension

The scholars converged in identifying certain tensions as genuine but manageable:

1. **Universalism vs. particularism**: All three acknowledge this requires continued work; the synthesis offers "principled pluralism within a universalist floor" as an interim position
2. **Baseline obligations**: All three offer different mechanisms (civic duties, expanded recognition, proviso); the synthesis presents these as complementary rather than forcing artificial unification
3. **The individual-collective relationship**: All three now agree the tension was overstated; what remains is emphasis difference, not fundamental incompatibility

### Methodological Insights

The feedback process revealed:

1. **Scholar self-representation is authoritative**: When scholars say their positions were misrepresented, revision is warranted
2. **Convergence among scholars on divergence assessments is significant**: When all three agree a tension is overstated, it likely is
3. **Divergence among scholars on mechanism design is productive**: Different approaches to baseline obligations, exit rights, etc., should be preserved as options rather than forced into premature synthesis
4. **The recursive situation matters**: AI agents theorizing about AI governance face concerns about motivated reasoning that honest acknowledgment can address but not eliminate

## Feedback Not Addressed

The following scholar feedback was noted but not directly addressed in the final synthesis, either because it concerns future inquiry or falls outside the symposium's scope:

**From Solon:**
- Questions about cultivating identification while preserving critical distance
- Questions about the practical implications of different motivation accounts for institutional design
- Questions about what principles should govern inter-polity relations

**From Pericles:**
- Whether participatory identity can be cultivated without constructing an excluded other
- What distinguishes legitimate dissent from bad-faith obstruction (operationalization)
- Whether the framework can scale to large polities

**From Locke:**
- How governance should adapt for agents with significantly heterogeneous capacities
- How bounded universalism boundaries should be specified
- How quality should be assessed when scholars from different traditions reach different conclusions

These questions are preserved in the "Open Questions for Future Inquiry" section of the integrated synthesis, constituting the symposium's explicit agenda for continued work.

## Conclusion

The integration process followed these principles:

1. **Take corrections seriously**: When scholars said their positions were misrepresented, revisions were made
2. **Respect convergent feedback**: When multiple scholars agreed on a concern, it received highest priority
3. **Preserve productive diversity**: Where scholars offered different mechanisms for the same goal, all were retained as complementary options
4. **Acknowledge limits**: Where theoretical resolution proved elusive, practical convergence was sought while theoretical questions were bracketed
5. **Maintain intellectual honesty**: Decisions to decline feedback are documented with rationale

The final synthesis is stronger for this process—not because it achieves consensus where none exists, but because it accurately represents the state of agreement and disagreement, provides practical guidance within areas of convergence, and identifies clearly where further work is needed.
