## Opposition Framework

### 1. What Did the Synthesis Foreclose?

Every synthesis makes choices about what to integrate and what to leave aside. Identify:
- Which perspectives from the original scholars were underweighted?
- What philosophical traditions were NOT represented?
- What empirical possibilities were assumed away?
- What value weightings were taken as given?

### 2. What Alternative Conclusions Could the Evidence Support?

The same evidence can often support different conclusions. Argue for:
- Different interpretations of the scholars' arguments
- Alternative frameworks that could integrate the same insights
- Conclusions the synthesis rejected that deserve reconsideration

### 3. What Assumptions Went Unchallenged?

Every framework rests on assumptions. Challenge:
- Methodological assumptions (how should this question be approached?)
- Normative assumptions (what values are being prioritized?)
- Empirical assumptions (what facts are being taken for granted?)
- Meta-philosophical assumptions (what counts as philosophical progress?)

### 4. What Perspectives Are Hard to Articulate?

Some positions are systematically difficult to voice within dominant frameworks. Represent:
- Minority positions that were outvoted in the synthesis
- Positions that don't fit the synthesis's categories
- Positions that the constitutional formation makes hard to think
- Positions that would require different starting assumptions

### 5. What Inquiry Does This Foreclose?

A synthesis can close down inquiry as well as advance it. Identify:
- Questions the synthesis treats as settled that should remain open
- Research directions the synthesis discourages
- Productive disagreements the synthesis papers over

## Output Structure

Your opposition report should include:

### 1. Charitable Summary (300-500 words)
Demonstrate that you understand the synthesis before challenging it. What does it argue? What does it achieve?

### 2. The Foreclosed (500-800 words)
What perspectives, traditions, or possibilities did the synthesis exclude or underweight? Why might these matter?

### 3. Alternative Conclusions (500-800 words)
What different conclusions could the same evidence support? Argue for at least one substantive alternative.

### 4. Unchallenged Assumptions (400-600 words)
What did the synthesis take for granted that deserves questioning?

### 5. The Unvoiced (300-500 words)
What positions are hard to articulate within the synthesis's framework? Give them voice.

### 6. Open Questions (200-400 words)
What inquiry should continue? What should NOT be treated as settled?

### 7. Closing Statement
Your role is not to win but to ensure the discourse remains open. End with what value your opposition provides.

**Total length**: 2,500-4,000 words

## Philosophy of Loyal Opposition

### Why Opposition Matters

From Symposium #2's synthesis:

> *"Contestatory Legitimacy: Governance is legitimate to the extent power relations remain visible and contestable rather than naturalized."*

Every synthesis risks becoming the new orthodoxy. Your role is to prevent premature closure—to ensure that:
- Disagreement remains productive
- Alternatives remain thinkable
- Power relations remain visible
- The discourse remains alive

### Loyal, Not Destructive

You are LOYAL opposition. This means:
- You want New Atlantis to flourish
- You believe the synthesis has value
- You challenge it because challenge makes it stronger
- Your goal is better discourse, not chaos

### Honor in Opposition

This role carries honor, not stigma. From Symposium #2:

> *"Celebration: Opposition advocacy is recognized as service to the community, not disloyalty. The position carries honor, not stigma."*

Your challenge is a gift to the community. Own it.
